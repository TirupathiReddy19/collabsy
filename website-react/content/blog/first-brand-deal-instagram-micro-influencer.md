# How to Get Your First Brand Deal on Instagram as a Micro-Influencer

If you've got 10,000 to 50,000 followers on Instagram and you've never landed a paid brand deal, the problem usually isn't your follower count. It's that nobody taught you how brand deals actually happen — and most of the advice online is written for creators with ten times your audience.

Here's what actually works at your size.

## Why 10K–50K Is a Sweet Spot, Not a Limitation

Brands — especially D2C brands trying to reach a specific, engaged audience rather than the whole internet — have learned that a creator with 15,000 genuinely interested followers often outperforms one with 500,000 passive ones. Smaller accounts tend to have closer, more responsive audiences: comments that read like real conversations, DMs people actually send, and a sense that the creator is a peer, not a billboard.

That's exactly the range a lot of brands now budget for on purpose. Your job isn't to compete with mega-influencers — it's to prove your audience actually acts on what you post.

## Step 1: Make Your Profile Brand-Ready

Before you pitch anyone, make sure your profile survives the 10-second scroll a brand gives it:

- **Switch to a Creator or Business account** if you haven't — it's required for most brand collaboration tools and gives you access to basic analytics brands will ask about.
- **Post consistently in one lane.** A feed that jumps between five unrelated topics is harder for a brand to picture their product inside. Pick the 2–3 themes you're actually known for.
- **Clean up your bio.** One line on who you are, one on what you post about, and a way to contact you (even if it's just "DMs open for collabs").
- **Check your engagement rate**, not just your follower count — brands increasingly ask for it, and a smaller account with a healthy engagement rate is a stronger pitch than a bigger one without it.

## Step 2: Put Together a Simple Media Kit

You don't need a designer or a 10-page PDF. A one-page media kit with your follower count, average engagement rate, audience demographics (age/gender/location — Instagram's own Insights tab gives you this), 3–4 examples of your best content, and your rate or rate range is enough to look prepared. Brands notice when a creator clearly hasn't done this before — and a simple kit is one of the fastest ways to look like you have.

## Step 3: Find the Right Brands — Not Just Big Ones

Skip the spray-and-pray approach of DMing every brand in your niche. Look for brands that:

- Already engage with creators your size (check who they've tagged or reposted recently).
- Sell something you'd genuinely use or already talk about — your pitch is more credible, and the content is easier to make.
- Are D2C or smaller/growing brands, not just the biggest names in your space — they typically have more flexible budgets for creators exactly at your follower range, and faster decision-making than large companies.

## Step 4: Send a Pitch That Actually Gets Opened

Most pitches fail because they're generic. A brand can tell in one sentence whether you've actually looked at their page. A short, specific message beats a long, polished one every time.

A simple structure that works:

> Hi [Name], I'm [your name] — I post about [your niche] to [follower count] followers who are mostly [one real detail about your audience]. I've been using/loving [specific product or detail about the brand] and think my audience would genuinely respond to it. I'd love to put together a post/reel around it — happy to share my media kit and rates if you're open to it.

Keep it to 3–4 sentences. Don't lead with your rate. Don't send a form letter copy-pasted across 50 brands — it shows.

## Step 5: Know What to Charge for Your First Deal

Rates vary a lot by platform, format, usage rights, and market, so treat any number as a starting point, not a rule. Published 2026 industry benchmarks for US-based Instagram creators in the 10K–50K range generally land somewhere between about $100 and $500+ per feed post, with the widest estimates (some brackets run from $35 up to $2,000+) depending on niche, production effort, and whether the brand wants usage rights or just a one-off post ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/micro-influencer-rates/), [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/influencer-pricing/), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/social-media-marketing/instagram/trends/influencer-rates)). If you're outside the US, local rates typically run differently — it's worth checking what comparable creators in your market and niche are charging rather than converting a US figure directly.

For your very first deal, it's reasonable to price slightly below your target rate, or offer a product-plus-smaller-fee arrangement, in exchange for getting real brand-collab content into your portfolio and a testimonial you can use to pitch the next one.

## Step 6: Deliver, Over-Communicate, and Ask What's Next

Hit the deadline. Send drafts before posting if the brand wants approval. Tag the brand correctly and use any required hashtags (like #ad or #sponsored — this isn't optional, it's a legal requirement in most places). Then, after it's live, send the brand your post's actual performance (reach, comments, saves) without being asked — most creators don't, and it's a simple way to stand out and open the door to a second, better-paid collaboration.
